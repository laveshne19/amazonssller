## GEO: getting picked up by Rufus and AI search

Rufus and AI search tools (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) read listings differently from the A9 algorithm. They look for direct answers to questions. What I did for that:

1. **Concrete numbers everywhere.** "30 hours", "0.13 inches", "112 languages" are things an AI can quote back. "Long battery life" isn't.
2. **Named use cases.** When someone asks Rufus "what's a good recorder for nursing lectures", it matches on use case words. The description lists who it's for in plain language.
3. **Honest limits in the copy.** The Bluetooth headset note and the call consent note answer questions AI assistants get asked a lot. Listings that answer them get cited more.
4. **Filled attributes.** Rufus leans on structured attributes heavily. The attribute table in `listing.md` covers the ones that matter.

### Q&A to seed (customer questions section or A+ FAQ module)

These are the questions people actually ask about AI recorders. Answering them on the listing gives Rufus a ready answer.

- **Does it record both sides of a phone call?** Yes. Attach it to the back of the phone with the magnetic ring and switch to call mode. It picks up the other person through the phone body.
- **Does it work with a Bluetooth headset?** Not for calls. Phone audio can't be captured while a Bluetooth headset is connected.
- **Is there a subscription?** It comes with 3 months of Pro free. After that there is a free starter plan, and paid plans if you need more minutes. **[check exact minutes]**
- **Can it do more than one language in a single recording?** One language per recording file. You can translate the transcript after.
- **Does it separate speakers?** Yes, the transcript labels speakers. It can mix them up when people talk over each other, which is true of every AI recorder.
- **Will it work without the app?** It records on its own. You need the app to transcribe and summarize.

## Things to fix before publishing

The brand's own pages disagree with each other in a few places. Pick the true value and use it everywhere, because inconsistent specs trigger Amazon catalog flags and hurt conversion.

| Item | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Languages | 112 (manual, meta title, Newegg) vs 145 (one bullet on the Recolx site). I used 112. |
| Warranty | 12 months (website) vs 18 months (manual). I used 12. |
| Standby | 60 days only appears in the manual. |
| Android | Never stated explicitly anywhere I could find. |
| Microphones | Not given in marketing. One comparison table says 2. |
| Free plan minutes | 300 per month according to one Recolx page, not stated on the Tap page. |
| Templates | 10,000+ on one page, 71 on another. I left the number out. |

## Claims I left out on purpose

- **"Military grade" encryption and "HIPAA compliant".** Amazon treats unverified compliance claims as restricted. Unless Recolx has a certificate to upload, these can get the listing suppressed. "Encrypted" is safe.
- **AI model names (GPT-5, Claude, Gemini).** These are other companies' trademarks, and the lineup changes often. Saying "AI" keeps the listing accurate when models change.
- **Hours or dollars saved.** Unsupported performance claims are a common reason for listing takedowns.
